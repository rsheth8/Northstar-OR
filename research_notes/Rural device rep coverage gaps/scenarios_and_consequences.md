# Scenarios and consequences when the device representative cannot attend in person

Scope: pacemaker/ICD (CIED) implants, checks and perioperative/ED interrogations affected by industry rep absence. Evidence labels: **[DATA]** = study/trial numbers; **[GUIDANCE]** = society or manufacturer documents; **[ANECDOTE/VENDOR]** = news, forum, or sponsored content. "Verified by fetch" means I read the page or document text. "Search snippet" means I saw the claim only in search output, so check it before quoting it on a slide.

## COVID-19 era: rep/vendor access restrictions and what happened to implants and checks

### Takeaway
In 2020, societies and manufacturers moved device checks to remote monitoring and to hospital staff, with reps giving support by phone or video. Implants kept going "as required", but urgent pacemaker implants fell during lockdowns and patients who came in were sicker. These studies tie the drop to patients presenting later, not to rep absence directly.

### Cited Findings
- **[GUIDANCE, verified by fetch]** Abbott Medical ANZ customer letter, 27 March 2020, aligned with the CSANZ position statement:
  - "Where possible all in-hospital device interrogations should be performed by hospital staff. Remote technical support will be provided by an Abbott representative, where required. This includes all post-operative day one checks, emergency department and ward interrogations."
  - "Emergency reprogramming will be facilitated by Abbott representatives in the absence of in-hospital support."
  - Reps would "step clinicians through device checks or programming" via "telephone or video/mobile technology e.g. FaceTime".
  - If a clinic needs urgent reprogramming and has no programmer, it "can be arranged with an Abbott representative at a suitable time". That means deferral.
  - Implants: Abbott staff "will continue to support implants … as required in line with hospitals' guidelines and protocols."
  - Source: [Abbott CRM Customer Letter COVID-19, 27 Mar 2020](https://dam.abbott.com/en-aus/documents/pdf/Abbott-CRM-Customer-Letter-COVID19-27-March-2020.pdf)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet]** HRS COVID-19 Task Force Message (20 Mar 2020) discouraged routine in-person device interrogations where remote monitoring could replace them, to cut exposures among patients, personnel and programmers. It also said to wipe programmers and wands between uses. — [HRS Task Force Message, 20 Mar 2020](https://hrsonline.org/documents/COVID19-TaskForceMessage-20March2020/download)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet]** HRS/ACC/AHA guidance (31 Mar 2020):
  - flagged exposure risk to "industry representatives" along with patients and staff;
  - sorted procedures into urgent (not postponed), semi-urgent and non-urgent/elective (postponed).
  - Sources: [HRS resource page](https://www.hrsonline.org/resource/guidance-cardiac-electrophysiology-during-covid-19-pandemic-hrs-covid-19-task-force-acc/); [ACC Ten Points](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/ten-points-to-remember/2020/03/30/12/17/guidance-for-cardiac-electrophysiology-covid); [PMC7598539](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7598539)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet]** HRS CIED management document: CIED interrogation should be done via remote monitoring rather than in person wherever possible. — [HRS COVID-19 CIED management](https://hrsonline.org/documents/covid-19-cied-managementpdf/download)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet]** Canadian Heart Rhythm Society used a risk-based approach; higher-risk patients were still seen in person. — [CHRS COVID-19 update](https://ccs.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/CHRS-COVID-19-Update-EN-FR.pdf)
- **[TRADE PRESS, search snippet; page would not load]** EP Lab Digest article on remote programming in the COVID era:
  - hospitals limited industry reps entering the hospital to conserve PPE;
  - many device clinics went remote-only;
  - Medtronic RemoteControl allowed real-time remote testing and programming of a CIED "without the need for clinical device specialists in the EP lab", introduced by the authors in April 2020.
  - Source: [EP Lab Digest](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/eplab/remote-programming-cardiac-implantable-electronic-devices-clinical-implications-era-covid-19)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** A Chinese 5G platform let device specialists do remote parameter testing and programming without entering the cath lab. — [Frontiers Cardiovasc Med 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2022.1010409/pdf)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Portuguese cross-sectional study (J Arrhythmia):
  - urgent pacemaker implants fell 23.7% during lockdown versus the same period in 2019;
  - implants rose 106.9% post-lockdown versus lockdown;
  - lockdown patients were 7.57 times more likely to present with hypotension or shock (p = .013).
  - Source: [Almeida Fernandes et al., J Arrhythmia](https://doaj.org/article/c86872b071cb44f09a0aba80420f96ef)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Melbourne-area hospital (Heart Lung Circ 2021): device implants fell sharply April–August 2020, then spiked in September 2020, which the authors read as likely delayed presentation. — [PMC8324176](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8324176)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Italian review: CIED implants declined generally during the pandemic; precise national before/after data were "still lacking". — [Intern Emerg Med 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-023-03450-1)

### Inferences
- The COVID period is the clearest documented natural experiment of "rep not physically present". The workaround each time was:
  1. hospital staff run the programmer;
  2. the rep supports by phone or FaceTime;
  3. otherwise, defer ("at a suitable time").
  This is a fragmented, context-poor support channel. The Northstar Remote Support Link is aimed at exactly this gap.
- The lockdown implant drop is attributed to patients delaying care, not to rep absence. Do not claim that rep restrictions caused worse outcomes.

### Gaps
- I found no study that quantifies delays, cancellations or adverse events attributable specifically to rep exclusion during COVID.
- I could not open the EP Lab Digest RemoteControl article to get case counts.

## Weather, travel and distance delaying device procedures or rep arrival

### Takeaway
**I found no documented news story or case report of a winter storm or road closure stopping a CIED rep from reaching a procedure.** The closest evidence:
- weather-driven rescheduling of elective procedures in Upper Midwest systems;
- an ED trial describing community EDs "waiting as the nearest company representative traveled to their location" on weekends and holidays.

### Cited Findings
- **[TRADE PRESS, verified by fetch]** EP Monthly: community EDs "sometimes struggled to have CIEDs interrogated on weekends and holidays, waiting as the nearest company representative traveled to their location". Interrogation with conventional programmers was limited to IBHRE-certified professionals, usually electrophysiologists or company reps. — [EP Monthly, Remote CIED Interrogator Usage in the ED](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)
- **[ANECDOTE/NEWS, search snippet; page returned 403]** Becker's Hospital Review on a March 15 snowstorm with blizzard-like conditions across the Upper Midwest (year not confirmed):
  - Aspirus Health (Wausau, WI) contacted patients to reschedule "elective or non-urgent procedures";
  - UP Health System–Marquette (MI) rescheduled elective procedures while staying open.
  - Source: [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/care-coordination/winter-storm-disrupts-care-in-2-states/)
- **[NEWS, search snippet]** Winnipeg Regional Health Authority postponed some elective surgeries during a December 2025 winter storm; EDs stayed open. — [WRHA winter storm updates](https://wrha.mb.ca/2025/12/18/winter-storm-updates/)
- **[NEWS, search snippet]** A storm disrupted health services across the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic (Winter Storm Blair). — [This Week Health](https://thisweekhealth.com/news/winter-storm-blair-disrupts-health-services-across-midwest-and-mid-atlantic/)
- **[REFERENCE TEXT, search snippet via Medscape]** Some pacemaker manufacturers provide courtesy interrogation services with site visits for rural populations that lack easy access to facilities. — [Medscape Pacemaker Syndrome Treatment](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/159706-treatment)

### Inferences
- The pattern is that EDs stay open and elective procedures get rescheduled. Scheduled implants or generator changes in a storm would plausibly fall into the "rescheduled" bucket. That is an inference, not a documented CIED case.
- The rural risk is mainly distance and travel time for one rep covering a wide territory. Weather adds to that, but I found no quantified data on it.

### Gaps
- No Minnesota, Alaska, northern Canada or Australia story found that specifically ties a weather or travel delay to a CIED rep.
- I did not search Star Tribune or MPR archives directly; a targeted archive search is still worth doing.

## Rep shortage, scheduling conflicts and waiting for a rep

### Takeaway
The only hard numbers on "waiting for the rep" come from ED interrogation trials. Standard rep- or programmer-based interrogation took about 2.5–3.5 hours (median 166.5–210 min), versus about 25 min to under 100 min with ED staff or remote transmitters. I found no published cancellation rate attributable to rep unavailability.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, RCT, search snippet; DOAJ page returned 403]** Neuenschwander et al., Clin Exp Emerg Med 2016 (doi 10.15441/ceem.15.118):
  - randomised, n = 60;
  - median time to interrogation: ED staff 98.5 min versus the standard process (company rep or hospital employee) 166.5 min;
  - ED length of stay did not differ: 249 (153–390) versus 246 (143–333) min, P = 0.71.
  - Source: [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/938332f9eb5840ba82ca0e853343f78e)
- **[DATA, registered trial results, search snippet]** REMEDY trial (NCT01871090, Abbott-sponsored, 2013–2015):
  - median time to interrogation: 25 min with an unpaired remote transmitter (n = 35) versus 210 min with a programmer (n = 48);
  - time measured from triage to the first of: interrogation done, treatment decision, or ED checkout.
  - Source: [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01871090](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01871090)
- **[TRADE PRESS, search snippet; page would not load]** EP Lab Digest "Management of the Device Patient Outside the Arrhythmia Center":
  - one hospital reported in-person industry rep response times to the ED "could often reach up to or greater than 150 minutes";
  - after a remote consult system, interrogation-to-analysis averaged 28 min.
  - Source: [EP Lab Digest](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/eplab/articles/Management-Device-Patient-Outside-Arrhythmia-Center)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet; not verified in full text]** 2023 HRS/EHRA/APHRS/LAHRS consensus on the remote device clinic:
  - reportedly cites a system that cut time to interrogation/interpretation by 78%, to a mean of 22 ± 14 min, "compared to calling for the local device representative to physically attend";
  - says these transmitters cannot reprogram;
  - says they could extend remote monitoring into rural and underserved areas.
  - **Check these numbers in the full text before using them.** — [Heart Rhythm 2023](https://www.heartrhythmjournal.com/article/S1547-5271(23)02011-8/fulltext)
- **[ANECDOTE/VENDOR, search snippet]** MedTech Dive sponsored content:
  - procedures are delayed when a rep "cannot physically get to a hospital in time";
  - conversely, reps wait hours when the surgeon runs late in another case.
  - This is vendor marketing, not data. — [MedTech Dive sponsored](https://www.medtechdive.com/spons/remote-collaboration-from-the-operating-room-the-new-normal-for-medtech/604936/)
- **[DATA, related but not rep-specific]** ECRI/ISMP survey: supply shortages caused surgical cases to be rescheduled, postponed or cancelled. This concerns products, not reps. — [MedTech Dive 2023](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/supply-disruptions-delaying-surgeries-ecri/697140/)
- **[ANECDOTE, forum]** A rural hospital nurse thread describes reliance on the company rep during business hours, with debate over whether bedside staff can interrogate. — [allnurses](https://allnurses.com/pacemaker-interrogation-t505892/)

### Inferences
- A wait of roughly 2.5–3.5 hours for rep-dependent interrogation, versus under 30 minutes remotely, is the most defensible "delay" number for a pitch. Cite it as ED interrogation, not OR/implant.
- These ED results did not shorten overall length of stay (RCT P = 0.71). The benefit is faster device information, not proven throughput or outcome gains. Do not overclaim.

### Gaps
- No data on implant or OR cases delayed or cancelled because a rep was absent.
- No data on reps covering simultaneous cases.
- No survey of EP labs on rep-related delay frequency was found.

## Emergency scenarios at small hospitals: urgent interrogation, perioperative management, after-hours help

### Takeaway
Most ED interrogations do not lead to urgent reprogramming (about 4% in one community-hospital series). However, perioperative reprogramming does need trained personnel or a rep, and guidance notes that this "may delay surgery". Magnets are a limited workaround: they give no asynchronous pacing in ICDs, and some leadless pacemakers have no magnet response.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, secondary citation verified by fetch]** EP Monthly reports a community-hospital series, 2015–2017:
  - 182 remote interrogations, of which "only seven (4%) found results requiring immediate device intervention";
  - the rest were normal or non-urgent, such as low battery.
  - The primary reference (ref 7) is missing from the article's list. — [EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Annals of Emergency Medicine abstract: pacemaker and defibrillator interrogations in the ED and hospital "rarely lead to device reprogramming". — [Ann Emerg Med abstract 102](https://www.annemergmed.com/article/S0196-0644(17)31028-4/fulltext)
- **[DATA, verified by fetch via EP Monthly]** Brand-mismatched remote interrogation safety:
  - 150 interrogations of 75 ex-vivo CIEDs (25 per major manufacturer) turned off or reprogrammed none;
  - only about 55% of ED patients brought their device ID card (Daugherty et al., JETS 2018, "45% … present without device identification cards").
  - Source: [EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet]** UpToDate, perioperative CIED management:
  - trained personnel must be present for preoperative reprogramming, "which may delay surgery";
  - adjustments are delayed while the programmer or trained staff return to the OR;
  - a magnet does not produce asynchronous pacing in any ICD;
  - pacing-dependent patients may become bradycardic or asystolic if electromagnetic interference inhibits pacing;
  - some leadless devices, e.g. Micra, have no magnet response.
  - Source: [UpToDate](https://www.uptodate.com/contents/perioperative-management-of-patients-with-a-pacemaker-or-implantable-cardioverter-defibrillator)
- **[GUIDANCE, search snippet]** 2011 HRS/ASA expert consensus on perioperative management:
  - lists contacting the manufacturer's rep to help interrogate before and/or after surgery, under physician direction;
  - notes that long electrosurgery bursts may inhibit pacing.
  - Source: [HRS/ASA 2011 full text](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2011-HRS_ASA-Perioperative-Management_FullText.pdf)
- **[CASE REPORT, search snippet]** Pacemaker patient having hernia repair under spinal anaesthesia: a pacemaker technician was available throughout and after surgery, and the team was told to avoid monopolar cautery. The surgery was uneventful. — [PMC13435945](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13435945/)
- **[TRADE PRESS, verified by fetch]** EP Monthly lists manufacturer phone lines (Abbott, Boston Scientific, Medtronic) for getting remote interrogators. It proposes, as a future research question, whether remote interrogators reduce CIED patient transfers at rural sites. — [EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)
- **[TRIALS]** Remote interrogation in rural EDs (NCT02421549) and CareLink Express versus traditional rep-based interrogation (NCT03223207) were registered to test time to interrogation. — [NCT02421549](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02421549); [NCT03223207](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03223207)

### Inferences
- After hours at a small hospital, the usual sequence is:
  1. call the manufacturer line or the rep;
  2. use a read-only or remote transmitter if one is available (no reprogramming);
  3. wait for the rep, or transfer.
- Because about 55% of patients carry their device ID card, the remote helper often has to establish the manufacturer and model first. This supports the "rep must ask which device" problem.

### Gaps
- No published rate of patients transferred solely for interrogation or reprogramming.
- EP Monthly explicitly lists this as unstudied.

## Communication gaps: remote help by phone, handoffs, documentation

### Takeaway
I found no study specifically on vendor/rep communication failures. General OR evidence is strong: about 30% of OR team exchanges had a communication failure, and about a third of those had visible effects, including delay and workarounds.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Lingard et al., Qual Saf Health Care 2004;13:330–334:
  - 90 hours of observation across 48 procedures and 94 team members;
  - 421 communication events, of which 129 were failures (about 30%);
  - failure types: occasion/timing 45.7%; content missing or inaccurate 35.7%; then purpose and audience (key people excluded);
  - 36.4% of failures had visible effects, "including inefficiency, team tension, resource waste, workaround, delay, patient inconvenience and procedural error".
  - Sources: [PubMed 15465935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15465935/); [AHRQ PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/communication-failures-operating-room-observational-classification-recurrent-types-and)
- **[DATA, verified by fetch via EP Monthly]** About 45% of CIED patients arrive at the ED without their device ID card. That is a missing-context problem for any remote helper. — [EP Monthly citing Daugherty et al. 2018](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)
- **[GUIDANCE, verified by fetch]** Abbott's COVID model moved support to "telephone or video/mobile technology e.g. FaceTime" to "step clinicians through device checks or programming". This is an ad hoc channel with no structured case context. — [Abbott 2020 letter](https://dam.abbott.com/en-aus/documents/pdf/Abbott-CRM-Customer-Letter-COVID19-27-March-2020.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lingard's "content" and "audience" failure types map directly onto a remote rep who joins mid-case without knowing which case, which device, what is done and what is unresolved. This is an analogy, not vendor-specific evidence.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Joint Commission sentinel-event communication statistics (often quoted as communication being a leading root cause). Source and verify them separately before citing.
- No study found on documentation gaps for rep-provided remote programming.

## Costs: OR/cath lab idle time, cancellations, transfers

### Takeaway
OR time costs about $36–37 per minute (California FY2014), so a 2.5-hour wait for a rep is on the order of $5,000 of OR time, if the room sits idle. Median helicopter air ambulance charges were about $36,400 in 2017.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Childers & Maggard-Gibbons, JAMA Surg 2018, using California FY2014 data:
  - mean OR cost about $36–37 per minute, of which about $20–21 is direct cost;
  - wages and benefits $13–14; supplies $2.50–3.50;
  - the authors caution that cutting minutes doesn't save the full amount.
  - Sources: [JAMA Surgery](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamasurgery/fullarticle/2673385); [PMC5875376](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5875376)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** A 2026 JAMA Surgery update (Ashrafi, Maggard-Gibbons, Childers) says earlier estimates are likely obsolete after COVID, inflation and consolidation. I did not obtain its figures. — [Citedrive record, doi 10.1001/jamasurg.2026.2355](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/costs-charges-and-revenue-of-hospital-operating-rooms-in-california/)
- **[DATA, search snippet]** Literature range cited in commentary: $7 to more than $100 per OR minute. — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/what-are-the-implications-of-the-costs-of-operating-room-time)
- **[DATA, GAO via news, search snippet]** Air ambulance median charges:
  - 2017 data: about $36,400 for helicopter, nearly $41,000 for fixed-wing (2012: about $22,000 and about $25,000);
  - two-thirds of about 34,000 transports examined were out-of-network.
  - Source: [NPR Illinois / KFF Health News, 2019](https://www.nprillinois.org/health-harvest/2019-06-14/why-air-ambulance-bills-are-still-sky-high)
- **[INDUSTRY STUDY, search snippet]** Commissioned by the Association of Air Medical Services:
  - average cost per transport $10,199;
  - Medicare reimburses an average 59%;
  - 75% of air medical transports are rural.
  - Source: [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/between-life-and-death-patients-left-with-little-choice-on-air-ambulance-costs-in-rural-nevada)

### Inferences
- Illustrative, not sourced: 166.5–210 min median wait × $36–37 per minute ≈ $6,000–7,800 of OR time, if an OR were held. The source waits are ED waits, so treat this as a hypothetical framing only.

### Gaps
- No cath lab or EP lab-specific per-minute cost found.
- No cost per cancelled CIED case found.
- No ground-ambulance interfacility transfer cost found.
