# CRM Device-Representative Coverage Today (pacemaker/ICD/CRT implants and device checks), with a rural Upper Midwest focus

Research date: 2026-10-25. All claims sourced inline. "Fact" = primary policy text or peer-reviewed data; "Opinion/anecdote" is labelled as such. Nothing here is clinical advice.

## 1. What a device rep does during an implant or check, and what they may not do (HRS 2008, HRS 2022, HRS/ASA 2011, AdvaMed)

### Takeaway
The governing professional text is the HRS policy statement on industry-employed allied professionals (IEAPs): Lindsay BD, Estes NAM III, Maloney JD, Reynolds DW, *Heart Rhythm* 2008;5:e8–e10, doi:10.1016/j.hrthm.2008.09.023. It was updated in 2022 by Haines DE et al. Under both versions the rep provides technical expertise on proprietary equipment at a physician's request and under the physician's supervision. That means running programmers and analyzers, interrogation, and support for testing and troubleshooting. The rep generally stays out of the sterile field, and every clinical interpretation and decision belongs to the physician.

### Cited Findings
**HRS 2008 IEAP policy statement (Lindsay et al.). Primary text read in full.** [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Background: it updates a 2001 NASPE statement (Hayes, Juknavorian, Maloney, *PACE* 2001;24:398–399). The update came out of an HRS Industry Forum in July 2007, attended by executives from Medtronic, Boston Scientific, St. Jude, Biotronik, Biosense Webster and Stereotaxis. The HRS Board endorsed it on September 10, 2008. "IEAP" covers "employed or contracted manufacturer representatives, field clinical engineers, and industry employed technical specialists." — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 1: the IEAP's role is "to provide technical expertise on the implant, use, and operation of their proprietary equipment … (including operation of programmers, analyzers, catheters, remote monitoring systems, mapping systems…)". Because IEAPs "are not licensed or authorized to practice medicine," this work happens "only at the request and under the direction of a qualified physician." — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 2: during an implant the IEAP operates "the programmers, analyzers, and other proprietary equipment under the supervision of the physician." As a rule the IEAP "should not enter the sterile field." Entry requires a specific written request from the physician and written approval from the institutional official who grants clinical privileges. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 3: office work requires *direct supervision*, meaning the physician is "present in the office suite and immediately available." — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 4: for an in-hospital device evaluation, the requesting physician must be "immediately available by phone" if not present. "It is the physician's responsibility to interpret the recordings and make any related clinical decisions." — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 6: IEAPs "should not be used as unpaid employees of a physician's practice." They should not routinely take on administrative work such as retrieving patient information or enrolling patients in remote monitoring. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 7: no clinical assistance when alone or unsupervised, and no technical help in a patient's home without a responsible physician. Remote-from-supervision help (for example, in a nursing home) is allowed only "under rare and urgent circumstances" where "the patient's condition and/or **distance from the medical facility**" would make delay harmful. It also needs a written, direct physician order (a verbal order followed by a written one is acceptable), and actions are limited to what the order specifies. This is the clause most relevant to rural sites. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 8: no support for a competitor's device, with two exceptions: an immediately life-threatening situation, or a device change, where the incoming company's IEAP may perform "routine" actions (interrogation, parameter printing, turning off the old device) under direct supervision. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 9: physicians are responsible for oversight and billing. "Medicare guidelines prohibit a physician from reporting the technical component of services performed primarily by an IEAP who is not employed by the physician." Incident-to billing must meet 42 CFR § 410.26. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Rec 10–11: IEAPs follow hospital policy, and "some hospitals may have policies that preclude IEAPs from some or all clinical activities." State, provincial or federal law takes precedence. Conduct should be consistent with the AdvaMed code. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Disclosure: one of the four authors (Reynolds) reported significant Medtronic relationships. — [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)

**HRS 2022 update (Haines DE, Austin C, Kusumoto FM, Liu CH, Mounsey JP, Proto-Conroy K, et al.; *Heart Rhythm*, online Oct 27 2022; doi:10.1016/j.hrthm.2022.08.029).** I did not get the full text; the publisher returned 403.
- It adds roles for mapping, imaging and LAAO specialists. It defines the "primary health care provider" the IEAP reports to, covers more practice settings, and adds a section on HIPAA-compliant data transfer and EMRs. — [HRS resource page](https://www.hrsonline.org/resource/hrs-policy-statement-update-recommendations-role-industry-employed-allied-professionals/); [PubMed 37264874](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37264874)
- In the MRI lab, the IEAP may "directly or remotely support device interrogation and reprogramming," and the primary provider supervises "directly or remotely." This explicitly accepts remote rep support in at least that setting. — [Heart Rhythm 2022 (search snippet)](https://www.heartrhythmjournal.com/article/S1547-5271(22)02377-3/fulltext)

**HRS/ASA 2011 perioperative consensus (Crossley et al.). Primary PDF read.** [HRS PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2011-HRS_ASA-Perioperative-Management_FullText.pdf)
- "It is not appropriate for the perioperative evaluation and prescription to be determined and delivered by an industry-employed allied professional." Manufacturer representatives "cannot be placed in a position of medical responsibility to provide independent prescriptive recommendations or independent postoperative CIED care. That is well beyond their scope of practice." An IEAP may still assist with "the technical part of that evaluation" under a physician experienced in CIED management. — [HRS/ASA 2011](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2011-HRS_ASA-Perioperative-Management_FullText.pdf)
- For emergency surgery, the algorithm reads "Contact manufacturer representative to assist in interrogation of device pre- and/or post-operative (under the direction of a physician knowledgeable in CIED function and programming)." — [HRS/ASA 2011](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2011-HRS_ASA-Perioperative-Management_FullText.pdf)

**AdvaMed Code of Ethics, section on technical support in the clinical setting (effective Jan 1 2020; read via law-firm summaries, not the code itself).**
- Reps are present only "at the request of and under the supervision of a qualified HCP." They must be transparent that they act for the company in a technical-support role and must not interfere with independent clinical decisions. They must follow facility privacy and credentialing policies. Their support "should not eliminate an overhead or other expense that the HCP otherwise would incur." The code is voluntary, not binding law. — [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/AdvaMed-Code-of-Ethics-Revisions-Effective-January-2020-Implications-for-Health-Care-Provider-Interactions-with-Vendors-04-25-2019); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/advameds_updated_code_ethics_0219.html)

**Practice description (opinion/anecdote).**
- A retired EP (EP Studios blog, Jan 30 2014) says reps run the programmer at implants, "never scrub in," and test and reprogram devices across the hospital "at least technically under supervision of a physician." He says reps take night call and are "expected to be available 24 hours 7 days a week." He also describes reps being asked for unnecessary pre-surgery reprogramming, emergency pace-termination, and ICD deactivation, and says supervising physicians sometimes leave programming decisions to reps. In the comments, a rep with 25+ years' experience describes pressure to complete physicians' op reports. — [EP Studios](https://www.epstudiossoftware.com/device-reps-and-patient-care-an-inconvenient-truth/)
- A 2020s JTD narrative review says industry device reps "frequently assist with interrogation and troubleshooting, particularly in urgent or after-hours settings," while the treating physician directs programming. — [J Thorac Dis review](https://jtd.amegroups.org/article/view/118511/html)
- HRS EP lab standards consensus: industry representatives "should function according to clear policies under the direction of the laboratory manager, staff, or physician." — [HRS EP Lab Standards PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Electrophysiology-Lab-Standards-Process-Protocols-Equipment-Personnel-and-Safety.pdf)

### Inferences
- During an implant, the rep's documented work maps onto programmer/analyzer operation, interrogation, measurement support for pacing threshold, sensing and impedance testing, troubleshooting, and printouts. The physician owns interpretation, programming decisions and the record. A product can support the coordination and documentation around these steps but should not frame rep output as a clinical decision.
- Rec 7 of the 2008 statement and the 2022 MRI-lab language together give policy cover for *remote, physician-ordered* rep support in limited cases. Distance is named as a legitimate factor.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full 2022 HRS update. Its exact implant-room and remote-support wording beyond the MRI section is unverified.
- I read the AdvaMed code text only through secondary summaries.

## 2. How dependent hospitals are on reps versus hospital-employed device staff

### Takeaway
Many sources describe reps as present for most implants and as the default after-hours resource. I found **no representative US survey that measures the share of implants or device checks supported by reps**. The evidence is qualitative, from small surveys, or about non-CRM specialties.

### Cited Findings
- EP Lab Digest: at many hospitals "an industry representative is present for most electrophysiology procedures." Reps supply devices and support testing and programming, which lets hospitals avoid keeping device inventory or hiring and training more EP lab techs. — [EP Lab Digest](https://www.eplabdigest.com/help-vaccinate-ep-industry-representatives)
- Lankenau Medical Center (EP Lab Digest interview, roughly 2012): "anywhere from 3 to 10 vendors in our EP lab each business day." — [EP Lab Digest spotlight](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/eplab/articles/spotlight-interview-main-line-health-lankenau-medical-center)
- Opinion: reps' services come at no direct cost, presumably priced into devices. Without them hospitals would have to hire technical staff "at considerable cost," so most hospitals prefer the status quo. — [EP Studios (2014)](https://www.epstudiossoftware.com/device-reps-and-patient-care-an-inconvenient-truth/)
- APSF: trained CIED specialists, including manufacturer reps, are increasingly hard to reach in emergencies, at night and on weekends. — [APSF](https://www.apsf.org/article/managing-cardiovascular-implantable-electronic-devices-cieds-during-perioperative-care/)
- ED study: checking a device often means calling industry reps or specially trained providers to bring readers, and readers are not cross-compatible between manufacturers, so the wrong call delays care. — [CEEM 2015](https://www.ceemjournal.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.15441%2Fceem.15.118)
- Italian multicenter study of Boston Scientific remote support (Bianchi et al., *J Innov Card Rhythm Manag* 2024;15:6070–6078): before the service, IEAPs were often present at scheduled follow-ups but rarely at unscheduled ones. The authors say "technical assistance is often lacking in clinical practice just when it is needed most." Two co-authors are Boston Scientific employees. — [PMC11573302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11573302/)
- Hospital-employed device workforce shortages: device programming is not taught in nursing or medical school. Vendors hire graduates at salaries clinics struggle to match, and new staff need 6–12 months to reach proficiency (vendor blog, Murj). — [Murj](https://www.murj.com/beyond-burnout-the-device-clinic-staffing-shortage-reality-and-how-to-overcome-it/). In an international survey of device clinic staff, staff shortage was the second-ranked challenge (33.8%). — [PMC13110432](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13110432/). Experienced specialists are retiring or moving into industry (vendor blog). — [CV Remote Solutions](https://cvremotesolutions.com/education-a-call-for-more-strengthening-the-device-clinic-workforce/)
- Analogues outside CRM: shoulder surgeons requested a rep for 52.5% of cases, and 78.6% said reps improved OR efficiency. — [JSES Int / PMC7846678](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7846678/). In a survey of 43 reps, 88% had given direct surgical instruction and 37% felt their involvement was excessive. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265392402_A_Survey_of_Healthcare_Industry_Representatives'_Participation_in_Surgery_Some_New_Ethical_Concerns)
- Howard & Hutchinson (2022) argue that staff increasingly depend on embedded industry technicians for device knowledge, which erodes in-house learning. — [PubMed 35476358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35476358)
- The 2015 HRS remote monitoring consensus recommends that industry "refrain from direct patient care" in clinic or home and makes remote monitoring the standard of care. — [HRS 2015 PDF](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2015-HRS-Remote-Interrogation-and-Monitoring.pdf)

### Inferences
- Small and low-volume implant sites are least able to fund IBHRE-certified in-house staff, so their dependence on reps is probably greatest. This is inferred, not measured.
- The mismatch is clear: scheduled cases get reps, while unscheduled, after-hours and perioperative needs often do not.

### Gaps
- I found no published share (%) of US implants or device checks covered by reps versus hospital staff, and no rural-specific figure.
- I found no count of IBHRE-certified professionals by state or by rural versus urban setting.

## 3. Scheduling, territory and travel, on-call, turnover, "rep fatigue"

### Takeaway
Public data on CRM rep territories and drive times are almost nonexistent. What exists is generic device-sales content, opinion pieces and manufacturer remote-support marketing. Manufacturers are building remote programmer support, which suggests in-person coverage is a recognized constraint.

### Cited Findings
- Generic device sales, not CRM-specific: a typical territory has 120–160 accounts and, in rural areas, can span "up to a couple thousand miles." Driving time is lost selling time. — [The Lobby Network](https://thelobbynetwork.com/articles/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-medical-device-sales-rep)
- Companies "often have to add $10K–$15K" to compensation to fill thin territories. The source describes this as anecdotal. — [MD Liaison 2026](https://mdliaison.com/medical-device-sales-salary-guide-2026)
- 24/7 call expectation for pacemaker reps (opinion, 2014). — [EP Studios](https://www.epstudiossoftware.com/device-reps-and-patient-care-an-inconvenient-truth/)
- **Manufacturer remote support (marketing or descriptive):**
  - Medtronic: CareLink SmartSync lets a support specialist work from the control room during an implant, with Bluetooth programming and screen sharing. The CareLink 2090 RemoteControl lets a Medtronic specialist run a real-time programming session from another location. WebEx is used for troubleshooting. — [Medtronic SmartSync](https://europe.medtronic.com/xd-en/healthcare-professionals/products/cardiac-rhythm/managing-your-patients/in-clinic-management/smartsync.html); [Medtronic remote programming options PDF](https://asiapac.medtronic.com/content/dam/medtronic-com/global/Corporate/covid19/documents/remote-programming-options.pdf). The PDF timed out when fetched, so details come from search summaries.
  - Abbott: Merlin 2 Remote Support / Merlin Live Connect lets an Abbott representative give programmer support to a local clinician. It was described as being in US limited market release at the time of the review. — [Front Cardiovasc Med 2022 / PMC9149410](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149410)
  - Boston Scientific: in a Heart Connect study at 6 Italian centers, 203 of 208 connections (98%) succeeded and 97% of visits were completed with remote support alone. Sessions averaged 6±4 min for a pacemaker and 8±5 min for an ICD. The remote user "cannot program or test the device," so a local operator does the hands-on work. — [PMC11573302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11573302/)
  - Biotronik: Renamic Neo "Live Support" provides real-time help from Biotronik specialists. — [search summary of PMC9149410 / related](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149410)
- Xiong et al. 2022 (3 cases, Abbott only): small hospitals often lack device specialists, and the authors note that remote programming "has not been officially approved for clinical use" in their setting (China). — [PMC9149410](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9149410)

### Inferences
- Remote support still needs a trained local operator, either a hospital clinician or a rep, at the patient, so it shifts the staffing problem rather than removing it.
- Any coverage-matching tool should treat "on-site rep," "remote rep support," and "hospital-employed device clinician" as distinct, explicitly labelled resource types.

### Gaps
- I found no published data on CRM rep territory sizes, headcount by state, drive times in Minnesota or northern regions, response-time SLAs, turnover, or burnout ("rep fatigue"). Searches of Cardiovascular Business, MedTech Dive and DAIC returned nothing specific.
- I found no manufacturer public statement of rural on-call commitments.

## 4. Regulatory and credentialing context

### Takeaway
Rep access runs through hospital vendor-credentialing programs, typically third-party systems such as Vendormate/GHX, Reptrax and symplr, and these are not standardized. Joint Commission expectations, HIPAA treatment provisions, Medicare billing rules and the voluntary AdvaMed code set the outer limits. I found no CMS Condition of Participation specific to device reps.

### Cited Findings
- Joint Commission (quoted by GHX): accredited organizations "need to be aware of who is entering the organization and their purpose." For OR reps, GHX's common-requirements guide lists time-limited approval, ID worn at all times, orientation, and training in HIPAA, sterile technique, attire and bloodborne pathogens, plus vaccinations. It cites ACS and AORN guidance. — [GHX common vendor credentialing requirements](https://www.ghx.com/media/pxibby0q/common-vendor-credentialing-rqmnts-0825.pdf)
- Credentials are not standardized across hospitals, so reps must get many different credentials (Becker's). — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/hospital-management-administration/3-issues-hospitals-and-vendors-face-regarding-vendor-credentialing/)
- Most systems use an outside credentialing company such as Reptrax or Vendormate (Relias 2013). Vendormate screens against OIG, DEA, GSA, OFAC and FDA lists. — [Relias Media](https://reliasmedia.com/articles/64721-salespeople-in-operating-room-pose-risks-require-vetting-and-guidelines)
- HIPAA (HHS FAQ 490): a covered provider may disclose PHI to a device company rep without authorization for its own treatment, payment or operations (45 CFR 164.506(c)), and when the device company itself acts as a provider. HHS's example is a rep in the OR, at the surgeon's request, giving support on "implantation, calibration or adjustment" of a device for that patient. — [HHS FAQ 490](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/490/when-may-a-covered-health-care-provider-disclose-protected-health-information-without-authorization/index.html)
- Medicare: physicians cannot bill the technical component of services performed primarily by a non-employed IEAP, and incident-to billing must meet 42 CFR § 410.26 (HRS 2008 Rec 9). — [HRS 2008](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)
- Liability (advocacy view): a Washington Legal Foundation piece argues that physicians request reps for technical support, not surgical assistance or evaluation of technique. — [WLF 2017](https://www.wlf.org/2017/04/21/publishing/medical-device-makers-representatives-in-the-o-r-patient-care-benefits-and-legal-ramifications/). An opinion piece adds that courts have held correcting a physician is not the rep's responsibility. — [EP Studios](https://www.epstudiossoftware.com/device-reps-and-patient-care-an-inconvenient-truth/)

### Inferences
- In a credentialed-vendor world, a rep who can technically cover a site may still be unable to enter it if their credential for that facility has lapsed. Credential status is therefore a real, separate coverage variable.

### Gaps
- I did not find or verify CMS CoP text (42 CFR 482) that addresses vendors directly. There are no FDA rules on rep presence, and FDA's role is device labeling and postmarket. I found no case law summaries specific to CRM reps.

## 5. Rural cardiology context (Minnesota, northern US)

### Takeaway
Rural areas have far fewer cardiologists, most implants happen in urban centers, and Minnesota's rural hospital network is dominated by small critical access hospitals (CAHs) that rely on outreach and transfer. I found no published count of Minnesota or US rural hospitals that implant pacemakers.

### Cited Findings
- 46.3% of US counties (22M people) have no practicing cardiologist, including 86.2% of rural counties (2024 JACC analysis, as reported). — [Cardiovascular Business](https://cardiovascularbusiness.com/topics/patient-care/care-delivery/cardiologist-shortage-here-no-heart-doctors); [Sickbay](https://sickbay.com/cardiology-deserts-rural-cardiac-care/)
- Minnesota has 76 CAHs per MDH (MHA says 77, each ≤25 beds). The Rural Health Information Hub also lists 1 Rural Emergency Hospital. — [MDH Flex CAH list](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/ruralhealth/flex/cah/index.html); [MHA](https://www.mnhospitals.org/resources/advocacy-center/resources-for-state-legislators/); [RHIhub MN](https://www.ruralhealthinfo.org/states/minnesota)
- Minneapolis Heart Institute (Allina) has 45+ rural and regional outreach sites across MN and western WI and reported 12,903 heart rhythm procedures in 2025, with no rural split. — [MHVI device clinic](https://www.mhvi.com/device-clinic/)
- North Shore Health (Grand Marais, MN) is a 16-bed CAH and the "only hospital for 80 miles." Parts of the county (Grand Portage, Gunflint Trail) are over an hour away, and medevac transfers to Duluth occur "several times a week." — [MN House submission 2025](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/fVLZj4CZj0ej1rUTFKiY5A.pdf); [North Shore Health](https://www.northshorehealthgm.org/about-us); [Duluth News Tribune](https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/opinion/columns/local-view-on-north-shore-were-fortunate-to-have-great-medical-care)
- National Inpatient Sample 2016–2020 leadless pacemakers: 28,340 urban versus 665 rural implants. Rural implants had lower adjusted complication odds but higher inpatient mortality, and rural patients are often transferred to urban centers. — [PMC11851073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11851073/)
- 1993–2009: rural hospitals used dual-chamber pacemakers less often (~72%) than urban non-teaching hospitals (~79%). — [JACC 2012](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2012.07.017)
- Medicare FFS 2016–2023: pacemaker and ICD implants are shifting toward outpatient and ASC settings. — [PubMed 40953741](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40953741/)
- Volume and outcomes: NCDR ICD Registry 2006–2008 (Freeman et al., JACC 2010), 224,233 implants at 1,201 hospitals. The lowest-volume quartile had adjusted OR 1.26 for any adverse event versus the highest. — [Cardiovascular Business](https://cardiovascularbusiness.com/node/1397); [HealthDay](https://consumer.healthday.com/cardiology-2/hospital-news-393/higher-volume-linked-to-fewer-adverse-events-with-icds-643252.html)
- Non-US analogue (New South Wales 2008–2018, *Global Heart* 2025): rural patients were less likely to get pacemakers (aOR 0.75) and CRT (aOR 0.72), and had higher 5-year mortality. — [PMC12700143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12700143/)
- 2023 HRS/EHRA/APHRS/LAHRS remote clinic consensus: remote monitoring extends reach into rural areas, but poor cellular or internet service can block transmissions. — [Heart Rhythm 2023](https://www.heartrhythmjournal.com/article/S1547-5271(23)02011-8/fulltext)
- Rural outreach clinics reduce patient travel, and modeling shows large access losses if cardiologist supply falls (*J Marketing* 2024). — [Bell, Lee, Gruca 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222429231207830)

### Inferences
- In northern Minnesota, a CAH would typically need device support for patients who already have implants (perioperative, ED, pre-transfer interrogation) more often than for new implants, which concentrate in regional and urban centers. This is inferred, not measured.
- Distance plus a single on-call rep per manufacturer per region makes after-hours and unscheduled needs the most fragile.

### Gaps
- I found no count of Minnesota or US rural or critical access hospitals that implant pacemakers, ICDs or CRT devices.
- I found no sourced data on winter road closures or seasonal travel delays on Hwy 61, the Iron Range or the Gunflint corridor, and no exact Grand Marais–Duluth drive time. A mapping service or MnDOT would be needed. I did not find Canadian (northern Ontario/Manitoba) rep coverage data.
