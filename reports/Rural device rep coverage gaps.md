# When the rep can't come, context breaks first

Rural pacemaker/ICD care depends on a manufacturer device rep who, by policy, only runs proprietary equipment under a physician's direction. When that rep can't get there, published practice falls back to the same few options every time: hospital staff run the programmer while the rep coaches by phone or FaceTime, a read-only remote check, a wait, a reschedule, or a transfer. The best "delay" numbers come from emergency departments, not ORs. Interrogations that depended on a rep or a programmer took a **median 166.5–210 minutes**, against **25–98.5 minutes** with remote transmitters or ED staff, and overall length of stay did not change. **No documented case shows a blizzard or road closure keeping a CRM rep from an implant.** What exists is weather-driven rescheduling of elective procedures in Upper Midwest systems, plus ED reports of "waiting as the nearest company representative traveled." Remote rep support is real but split by manufacturer: Medtronic SmartSync/CareLink, BIOTRONIK LiveSupport, Boston Scientific Heart Connect and Abbott Merlin each serve their own hardware, and every one of them carries video, audio and the programmer screen without structured case state. That gap is where Northstar fits honestly. It offers explainable logistical coverage ranking, a record of what the team confirmed or deferred, and a late-join brief with snapshot-anchored questions for the remote rep. Its limits are just as concrete: transport works only on a shared local network, named-rep acceptance isn't built, weather is a fixture, and there is no evidence for travel or cost savings. The pitch should present Northstar as the coordination and context layer that sits beside manufacturer programming channels, and should not claim it replaces them.

## Policy makes the rep a technician, and distance is the named exception

The governing text is the Heart Rhythm Society's 2008 statement on industry-employed allied professionals, endorsed by an industry forum that included Medtronic. Reps provide "technical expertise on the implant, use, and operation of their proprietary equipment," **only "at the request and under the direction of a qualified physician."** They generally stay out of the sterile field, and "it is the physician's responsibility to interpret the recordings and make any related clinical decisions" ([HRS 2008](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2008-HRS-Role-of-IEAP.pdf)). Recommendation 7 matters most for rural sites. It allows help away from direct supervision only "under rare and urgent circumstances" where the patient's condition "and/or **distance from the medical facility**" makes delay harmful, and only with a written physician order. The 2022 update goes further in the MRI lab, where the rep may "directly or remotely support device interrogation and reprogramming" ([HRS 2022 resource page](https://www.hrsonline.org/resource/hrs-policy-statement-update-recommendations-role-industry-employed-allied-professionals/); [Heart Rhythm 2022](https://www.heartrhythmjournal.com/article/S1547-5271(22)02377-3/fulltext)). The HRS/ASA perioperative consensus is blunter. Reps "cannot be placed in a position of medical responsibility to provide independent prescriptive recommendations," but its emergency-surgery algorithm still says to "contact manufacturer representative to assist in interrogation" ([HRS/ASA 2011](https://www.hrsonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2011-HRS_ASA-Perioperative-Management_FullText.pdf)).

Policy limits the rep's role, yet in practice hospitals rely on reps heavily. EP Lab Digest reports that at many hospitals "an industry representative is present for most electrophysiology procedures" ([EP Lab Digest](https://www.eplabdigest.com/help-vaccinate-ep-industry-representatives)). One retired electrophysiologist describes reps as expected to be "available 24 hours 7 days a week" ([EP Studios](https://www.epstudiossoftware.com/device-reps-and-patient-care-an-inconvenient-truth/)). The alternative workforce is thin. Device programming isn't taught in nursing or medical school, and in an international survey device clinic staff ranked staff shortage as their second-biggest challenge, at 33.8% ([PMC13110432](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13110432/)). The mismatch is sharpest at unscheduled moments. A Boston Scientific–coauthored Italian study found reps often present at scheduled follow-ups but rarely at unscheduled ones, and concluded that "technical assistance is often lacking in clinical practice just when it is needed most" ([PMC11573302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11573302/)). Entry to the building is a separate constraint. Access runs through third-party vendor credentialing that isn't standardized across hospitals, so a qualified rep with a lapsed credential at a given facility still can't walk in ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/hospital-management-administration/3-issues-hospitals-and-vendors-face-regarding-vendor-credentialing/); [GHX](https://www.ghx.com/media/pxibby0q/common-vendor-credentialing-rqmnts-0825.pdf)).

The rural picture makes distance real, though nobody has measured it well. **86.2% of rural US counties have no practicing cardiologist** ([Cardiovascular Business](https://cardiovascularbusiness.com/topics/patient-care/care-delivery/cardiologist-shortage-here-no-heart-doctors)). Minnesota has 76 critical access hospitals ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/ruralhealth/flex/cah/index.html)). North Shore Health in Grand Marais, the setting of the team's demo story, is a 16-bed CAH and the "only hospital for 80 miles," with medevac transfers to Duluth "several times a week" ([MN House submission](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/fVLZj4CZj0ej1rUTFKiY5A.pdf)). Implants are concentrated in cities: 28,340 urban versus 665 rural leadless pacemaker implants in 2016–2020 ([PMC11851073](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11851073/)). So a northern CAH probably needs rep help more often for patients who already have devices (perioperative, ED or pre-transfer interrogation) than for new implants. That is an inference. No source counts rural implanting hospitals, rep territories, drive times or response-time commitments.

## What actually happens: coach by phone, wait 2.5–3.5 hours, reschedule, or transfer

COVID is the clearest documented case of "rep not physically present." Abbott's March 2020 letter told hospitals that "where possible all in-hospital device interrogations should be performed by hospital staff," with reps who would "step clinicians through device checks or programming" by "telephone or video/mobile technology e.g. FaceTime." Urgent reprogramming without a programmer would be "arranged with an Abbott representative at a suitable time," which in practice means deferral ([Abbott 2020](https://dam.abbott.com/en-aus/documents/pdf/Abbott-CRM-Customer-Letter-COVID19-27-March-2020.pdf)). Urgent pacemaker implants fell 23.7% during Portugal's lockdown, and the patients who did come were sicker ([J Arrhythmia](https://doaj.org/article/c86872b071cb44f09a0aba80420f96ef)). Those studies attribute the drop to patients delaying care, not to reps being absent.

Weather has no direct evidence behind it. Becker's reports that during an Upper Midwest snowstorm, Aspirus Health and UP Health System–Marquette rescheduled "elective or non-urgent procedures" while staying open ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/care-coordination/winter-storm-disrupts-care-in-2-states/)), and Winnipeg postponed elective surgeries during a December 2025 storm ([WRHA](https://wrha.mb.ca/2025/12/18/winter-storm-updates/)). The closest thing to a rep-specific travel delay is EP Monthly's description of community EDs that struggled on weekends and holidays, "waiting as the nearest company representative traveled to their location" ([EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)). The blizzard scenario is therefore *plausible and representative*, but it is not documented.

The ED waiting numbers are the strongest quantitative evidence, and they come with an important caveat:

| Setting | Rep/programmer-dependent | Alternative | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ED RCT, n=60 | 166.5 min median to interrogation | 98.5 min (ED staff) | [CEEM 2016](https://doaj.org/article/938332f9eb5840ba82ca0e853343f78e) |
| REMEDY (Abbott-sponsored) | 210 min (programmer) | 25 min (remote transmitter) | [NCT01871090](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01871090) |
| Single hospital (trade press) | ≥150 min rep response | 28 min after remote consult | [EP Lab Digest](https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/eplab/articles/Management-Device-Patient-Outside-Arrhythmia-Center) |

The caveat is that faster interrogation did **not** shorten ED length of stay in the RCT (249 vs 246 min, P = 0.71). And only 7 of 182 (4%) community-hospital remote interrogations found anything that needed immediate intervention ([EP Monthly](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)). The demonstrated gain is faster device information, not better outcomes. The perioperative situation is tighter. UpToDate notes that trained personnel must be present for preoperative reprogramming, "which may delay surgery," and that magnets are only a partial workaround: a magnet does not produce asynchronous pacing in any ICD, and some leadless devices have no magnet response ([UpToDate](https://www.uptodate.com/contents/perioperative-management-of-patients-with-a-pacemaker-or-implantable-cardioverter-defibrillator)).

Remote helpers also start without basic context. **About 45% of CIED patients arrive at the ED without their device ID card**, so the first remote question is often which manufacturer and model the patient has ([EP Monthly citing Daugherty 2018](https://epmonthly.com/article/remote-cied-interrogator-usage-in-the-emergency-department/)). General OR research makes the same point. In Lingard's observational study, about 30% of team exchanges failed, mostly on timing (45.7%) or on missing or inaccurate content (35.7%), and 36.4% of failures had visible effects such as delay and workarounds ([PubMed 15465935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15465935/)). No study looks specifically at communication between reps and the OR, so applying Lingard here is an analogy.

Costs can only be illustrated, not proven. OR time averaged about **$36–37 per minute** in California FY2014, and the authors themselves caution that cutting minutes doesn't save the full amount ([JAMA Surgery 2018](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamasurgery/fullarticle/2673385)). Median helicopter air ambulance charges were about **$36,400** in 2017 ([NPR Illinois/KFF](https://www.nprillinois.org/health-harvest/2019-06-14/why-air-ambulance-bills-are-still-sky-high)). Multiplying an ED wait by an OR rate (roughly $6,000–7,800) mixes settings, so it is a hypothetical. Nobody has published the cost of a cancelled CIED case, an EP-lab cost per minute, or how often patients are transferred only for interrogation. EP Monthly lists that last question as unstudied.

## Remote support exists in four silos, all video- and screen-centric

Every major CRM manufacturer now supports implants remotely in some form, but each works only with its own programmer and its own staff. Medtronic's iPad-based SmartSync is FDA-approved for programming ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/medtronic-gets-fda-approval-for-wireless-pacemaker-programmer/554023/)). Regional marketing describes support "from the control room" and a "real-time, full programming session from a remote location," but that is not confirmed as a US claim. BIOTRONIK LiveSupport logged **38,943 sessions on 2,831 programmers from April 2022 to April 2025**, averaging 89 per weekday and 14 per weekend day. Staff *estimated* more than 60 minutes saved per patient in 78% of sessions. Four authors are BIOTRONIK employees, and the remote-site providers gave "mixed" answers on whether they preferred it to an in-person rep ([PMC12914562](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12914562)). Boston Scientific's Heart Connect is screen-share only. At six Italian centers, 98% of connections succeeded and 97% of visits were completed with remote support alone, but the remote user "cannot program or test the device" ([PMC11573302](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11573302/)).

The best independent evidence is one RCT: 480 patients in Chengdu with Abbott devices. Remote parameter testing during implant was non-inferior, with procedure time unchanged (102 vs 100 min) and median door openings falling from 6 to 0 ([Front Cardiovasc Med 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cardiovascular-medicine/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2024.1364940/full)). It was a single center on a research 5G platform. Across all these studies a trained person still has to be at the bedside to handle the wand and the programmer. Remote programming moves the rep's keystrokes, not their hands, so the staffing problem shifts rather than disappears. Connectivity is also a documented barrier: IT restrictions and poor cellular coverage hindered LiveSupport setup.

Generic OR telepresence has converged on video, audio and annotation. The best-funded rep-focused company, Avail Medsystems, raised about $125M for free OR consoles paid for by device-maker subscriptions ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/avail-medsystems-raises-100m-medtech-reps-remotely-oversee-surgeries/586636/)) and **shut down abruptly in November 2023** ([MassDevice](https://www.massdevice.com/avail-medsystems-abruptly-shuts-down/)). Explorer Surgical's "digital procedural playbooks" are the only structured-workflow precedent, and they target orthopedics and product launches rather than CRM ([Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/orthopedics-tjr/explorer-surgical-launches-new-remote-case-support-features/)). None of the CRM sources describe sharing case status, checklist progress or deferred items with the remote rep. That is the white space, but it is inferred from what the sources leave out. No study names it.

## Northstar closes the context gap and leaves the distance gap open

Measured against those realities, Northstar's implemented features line up with three of the failure points and miss one. The **Coverage Matcher** turns the after-hours scramble into an explicit, explainable ranking. It checks role, device family, availability, recorded drive time, setup buffer and simulated road closure, gives each rep a plain-language reason, and appends Reschedule when nobody can attend in person. "It ranks; it never commits a plan." In the demo, Ana's 110-minute drive arrives after the simulated highway closure, so the options become remote support from Ana or Marcus, or Reschedule ([CoverageMatcher.swift](../NorthstarOR/Features/Planner/CoverageMatcher.swift); [DEMO_WORKSPACE.md](../docs/DEMO_WORKSPACE.md)). Keeping "can support remotely" separate from acceptance, and keeping availability separate from clinical coverage, matches the literature's point that on-site rep, remote rep and hospital device clinician are different resources. **Co-pilot recording** keeps the team as the recording authority, with explicit confirmations, deferrals, a pinned required acknowledgment and retained corrections. That preserves unresolved state, which is exactly what is lost when support moves to an ad hoc FaceTime call. **Remote Support** gives a late-joining rep a full read-only projection of the room before any question. Each question carries an immutable context snapshot (case, site, device family, selected item, checklist lines, recent events), delivery states are distinct, and acknowledgment and reply are explicit. Basic two-way audio and room-camera video were owner-confirmed on October 25, 2026 ([NATIVE_DEMO_STATUS.md](../docs/NATIVE_DEMO_STATUS.md)). This structure maps onto handoff evidence. I-PASS cut medical errors 23% with structured handoffs, though at only 6 of 9 sites ([AHRQ PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/resources/resource/28485)), and TeamSTEPPS emphasizes receiver acknowledgment ([AHRQ](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/handoff.html)). These studies support the design principle. They do not validate Northstar.

The gap Northstar leaves open is the defining one. **The transport is nearby-only**: an encrypted Multipeer session on the same Wi-Fi, with Local Network permission. A rep stuck behind a closed highway is, by definition, not on the hospital LAN. Hospital guest SSIDs also commonly isolate clients and confine Bonjour discovery ([Cisco Meraki](https://documentation.meraki.com/Wireless/Design_and_Configure/Configuration_Guides/Client_Addressing_and_Bridging/Bonjour_Forwarding)), so pairing could fail even inside the building. That is untested. A real version would need an internet relay with TURN, a BAA-backed transport (OCR's COVID video enforcement discretion ended in 2023 ([HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/covid-19-phe-ends-may-11-2023/))), an audio/text fallback for thin rural links, and hospital consent policy for room video. The design already suits low bandwidth well, because the text and snapshot channels carry the context and only video needs heavy bandwidth. Other gaps per the repository's own audit: named-rep request, acceptance and rescheduling aren't implemented in Planner, even though PRODUCT.md and DEMO_READINESS.md script "Ana … explicitly accepts" (follow the status doc). There is no authentication or verified identity. Weather is a fixture `StormForecast` with one closure time, where a real version would take timestamped NWS alerts and MnDOT 511 closures as advisory inputs ([NWS API](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api)). Voice, AI and vision are not wired. The ICD template is intentionally blocked. Mute, camera flip and repeated-call recovery have not been physically verified. On regulation, Northstar has no patient fields and ranks logistics rather than clinical options, so it sits closer to the administrative-software exclusion of Cures Act §520(o)(1) than to clinical decision support ([FDA guidance](https://fda.gov/media/132527/download)). Adding label reading or AI case summaries would move it toward that boundary. The CDS guidance was revised in January 2026 ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software)).

Avoid three further claims: that Northstar uses live weather (it is a fixture), that it has verified identity, and that it records media. And do not use the widely repeated "80% of serious errors involve handoffs" figure, which has no primary Joint Commission source.

## Conclusion

The research changes what Northstar's problem actually is. Distance and weather are the trigger, but the documented harm is a fragmented, low-context fallback: whoever is in the room runs a manufacturer programmer while a rep coaches over a generic phone or video call with no shared record of the case. Manufacturers are solving the keystroke problem one brand at a time, and telepresence vendors are solving the video problem. Nobody has published a solution for the context problem. That is a defensible "Connect & Intersect" story for Medtronic, because Northstar sits where the room, the rep and the coverage plan meet, without competing with CareLink.

The honest next step is narrow and easy to check. Swap the transport for an internet relay with an audio/text fallback, wire named-rep acceptance, and feed the matcher timestamped NWS and 511 inputs. Then measure what nobody has measured: how long a remote rep takes to get oriented with a structured brief versus a cold call. A five-minute pitch that names those limits and points at that experiment will be more credible to Medtronic judges than any savings figure the team could invent.
